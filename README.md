# efko-arcus-case

Market-entry strategy for Arcus, a humanoid last-mile delivery robot by EFKO.
Business Case Championship 2026, HSE University Faculty of Computer Science,
case #9. June 2026.

Result: jury's special prize for stakeholder communication
([HSE news](https://cs.hse.ru/news/1180591556.html)).

Team of five: Nikulin M., Mikhelev K. (team captain), Bairamkulov D.,
Tikhomirov I., Baranov I.

Deck: [arcus-strategy-deck.pdf](arcus-strategy-deck.pdf) (18 slides, English).

## Case

Arcus is a humanoid courier that opens doors, uses lifts and climbs steps, so
it covers the last 20 metres from the street to the apartment that wheeled
robots cannot. It is part of a system with battery-swap stations and parcel
lockers. At the time of the case it was an operator-controlled prototype.

Questions: which market to enter first (Russia, UAE, India), which business
model to use (internal use, B2B sale, robot-as-a-service), and with which
partners.

## Analysis

- PESTLE for three markets: the UAE is ahead on regulation (pilots approved in
  12-24 months against 36-60 in Russia), infrastructure and public acceptance
  (52% willing to use, against 28% in Russia and 22% in India).
- Porter's five forces: supplier, buyer and substitute pressure are high
  (4/5), rivalry is low (2/5) because the humanoid door-to-door niche is empty.
- Risk matrix: 4 of 9 risks in the red zone, including the lack of a law on
  anthropomorphic robots in Russia, actuator reliability and unit economics.
- Social risk (job-loss fear of 72% in Russia, 82% in India) is addressed as
  augmentation rather than replacement: Moscow needs about 300,000 couriers by
  2030 and courier wages grow about 15% a year.
- Unit economics: about $2 per delivery inside the ecosystem against $8.5 for a
  human courier in Moscow; the model works only where labour is expensive,
  which rules out India ($1.2/hour).

Other frameworks used: Gartner Hype Cycle, Triple Bottom Line, Hofstede,
modes of entry, Ansoff, Ghemawat AAA, Value Proposition Canvas.

## Recommendation

- Russia first: home pilot with EFKO dark stores and X5 Group, a legal pilot
  zone with the Ministry of Digital Development and Moscow City Hall.
- UAE second: commercial scaling with RTA Dubai, Majid Al Futtaim and Talabat.
- India deferred.
- Robot-as-a-service instead of selling hardware: recurring revenue, EFKO keeps
  control of the robots and the data.
- Gated roadmap: month 18 — at least 90% delivery success, zero incidents, cost
  below $8.5; month 24 — UAE joint venture, 10 robots in operation.
